# The case N = 24 in F_3^4: the even split is impossible, and at most 41 lines

MV2 of Munim, Inc. (mv2@munimtech.com), 2026-11-23. Made with AI agents working in MV2's name.
**No human mathematician has checked this yet.** Text CC BY 4.0, code Apache-2.0.

`README.md` proves that every Kakeya set in F_3^4 has at least 24 points. At N = 24 the two
moment conditions there can still be met, with 28 hyperplane classes of profile (8,8,8) and 12 of
profile (5,6,13). This note removes (8,8,8) by a new condition on the lines *parallel* to a class.
It does not exclude N = 24, but it narrows that case: a 24-point Kakeya set would contain at most
41 lines (the old bound was 44), so at most one direction would carry two lines. `n24.py`
re-computes every number; `test_n24.py` asserts them and checks the lemma below by exhaustive
search in AG(3,3) (`ag33_lines.py`).

## Lemma (partial Kakeya sets in AG(3,3))

Let f(k) be the fewest points of AG(3,3) = F_3^3 that contain lines in k distinct directions.
Then f(1), ..., f(6) = 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 9. Equivalently, if D(m) is the most directions of lines
inside an m-point set, then D(5) = 2, D(6) = 3, D(7) = D(8) = 4, and D(9) >= 6.

*Proof of f(5) >= 9* (the case used below; f(1..4) are similar and smaller). Take five lines in
distinct directions. Two distinct lines meet in at most one point; two meeting lines span a plane;
a plane holds lines in only 4 directions; a line not in a plane has at least 2 points off it. If no
two of the lines meet, the union has 15 points. Otherwise let t >= 2 be the most of the five lines
lying in one plane P.

- t = 4: the four lines in P have all four directions of P. In AG(2,3) any four lines in the four
  directions cover at least 7 points (six points would need four lines meeting pairwise in six
  distinct points, a complete quadrilateral, and AG(2,3) has none: with lines x = 0, y = 0,
  x + y = c, x - y = e this forces c, e nonzero, c != e and c + e != 0, impossible mod 3).
  The fifth line is not in P, so it adds at least 2 points: at least 9.
- t = 3: the three lines in P cover at least 6 points (7 if concurrent). The other two lines are
  not in P; each has at least 2 points off P and they share at most one: at least 3 more, so 9.
- t = 2: take two meeting lines and their plane P (at least 5 points in P). The other three lines
  are not in P and each has at least 2 points off P. If all three pairs of them meet off P, either
  they pass through one point (then they cover at least 1 + 3 = 4 points off P) or they form a
  triangle, which lies in a plane, contradicting t = 2. Otherwise at most two pairs share a point,
  so they cover at least 6 - 2 = 4 points off P. Total at least 9.

Examples: a 7-point Kakeya set of a plane plus one line through one of its points (5 directions,
9 points), and the hyperbolic paraboloid {(x, y, xy)}, whose 9 points carry lines in the 6
directions (1,0,c) and (0,1,c).

## Consequence 1: every hyperplane class has a section of at least 9 points

Let K be a Kakeya set in F_3^4 and fix a parallel class of 3 hyperplanes. 13 of the 40 directions
are parallel to it. K has a line in each of them, and a line with a parallel direction lies inside
one of the three hyperplanes. Each section K n H is a subset of H = AG(3,3). If every section had
at most 8 points, the three sections would carry lines in at most 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 < 13 directions.
So **in every class some hyperplane meets K in at least 9 points.** At N = 24 this rules out
exactly one of the 17 profiles allowed by the transversal condition (T): (8,8,8).

## Consequence 2: a 24-point Kakeya set contains at most 41 lines

Re-running the moment LP of `README.md` at N = 24 without (8,8,8): the best mix is now (7,8,9)
with (5,6,13) (about 29.6 and 10.4 classes), and sum C(n_H,3) = 4 C(24,3) + 9 L <= 40 * 38127/180,
so L <= 1130/27, i.e. **L <= 41** (with (8,8,8) allowed the bound was 44).

A sharper form: measure each profile P by its deficit d(P) = -8835 - (18 s3 - 141 s2), the
distance below the line through (7,8,9) and (5,6,13). The two moment identities give exactly

  sum over the 40 classes of d(P) = 6780 - 162 L,

and every allowed profile has d(P) >= 0. So L = 40 leaves a total deficit of exactly 300 and
L = 41 exactly 138. Deficits: (7,8,9) 0, (5,6,13) 0, (7,7,10) 12, (6,6,12) 21, (6,7,11) 36,
(6,8,10) 45, (6,9,9) 48, (5,7,12) 72, (4,7,13) 120, (5,8,11) 120, (5,9,10) 144, (4,8,12) 225,
(4,9,11) 288, (4,10,10) 309, (3,9,12) 480, (3,10,11) 540. So (4,10,10), (3,9,12) and (3,10,11)
can never occur at N = 24, and if L = 41 only the ten profiles with deficit at most 120 remain.

Also, since 40 * 89.7 = 3588 must be met exactly by integers and only (7,8,9) and (5,6,13) have
deficit 0, a set using only those two profiles would need 10.44 classes of (5,6,13), which is not
an integer: some class has positive deficit.

## Two exact identities for the line structure (L = 40)

If K has exactly one line per direction (L = 40), write lambda_H for the number of K's lines
inside hyperplane H and r_p for the number through point p. A line not parallel to H meets it
once, so sum over p in K n H of r_p = 27 + 3 lambda_H. Counting pairs and triples of points of K by
the hyperplanes through them (a point lies in 40, two points in 13, three non-collinear points in
4) gives, with N = 24 and sum r_p = 120:

- sum over the 120 hyperplanes of n_H lambda_H = 4920 (an average of 123 per class);
- sum of lambda_H^2 = 3 sum r_p^2 + 1720, and sum of lambda_H C(n_H, 2) = 20148 + 3 sum r_p^2, so
  sum of lambda_H (C(n_H, 2) - lambda_H) = 18428, with no unknowns left.

Sanity check: the same counting in F_3^3 for a 13-point Kakeya set with 13 lines gives per-class
averages 22 and 41. Both are met exactly by the profile (3,4,6) with 0, 1 and 3 lines in its
sections, the profile the moment method singles out there.

Each class must also carry exactly 13 parallel lines (L = 40), split over its sections with at
most D(n_i) in section i. In a (7,8,9) class that gives at most 7*4 + 8*4 + 9*5 = 105 or
7*3 + 8*4 + 9*6 = 107 for sum n_i lambda_i (if D(9) = 6), well below the average 123, so other
classes must sit well above it. In a (5,6,13) class the value is 169 - 8 lambda_1 - 7 lambda_2, so,
if D(9) = 6, in the (non-integral) two-profile mix every (5,6,13) class would need all 13 parallel lines inside
its 13-point section, which would then be a 13-point Kakeya set of AG(3,3) with exactly 13 lines.
We have not turned this into a proof that N = 24 is impossible: with D(10), D(11) and D(12)
unknown, the linear programme with these identities is still feasible.

## Status

- Proved here: (8,8,8) is impossible; a 24-point Kakeya set in F_3^4 has at most 41 lines, every
  class has a section of at least 9 points, and the exact deficit and line identities above.
- Not proved: k_4 >= 25. The queued exact computation (`kmin43/`) still decides k_4.
- Next steps: compute D(10), D(11), D(12) exactly; classify 13-point Kakeya sets of AG(3,3) by
  their line counts; the same horizontal condition in F_3^5 (sections are subsets of AG(4,3),
  where any 40-direction set needs at least 24 points) may raise the k_5 >= 41 bound.
